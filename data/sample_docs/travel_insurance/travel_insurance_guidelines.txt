=============================================================
Applicable Authority: IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India)
Company Reference: ICICI Lombard General Insurance
Document Version: 2024-25 Edition

CHAPTER 1: TYPES OF TRAVEL INSURANCE
---------------------------------------

1.1 International Travel Insurance
Covers risks associated with international travel including:
- Medical emergencies and hospitalization abroad
- Emergency medical evacuation and repatriation
- Trip cancellation and interruption
- Loss/delay of checked baggage
- Loss of passport
- Personal accident (death or disability during travel)
- Personal liability coverage abroad
- Hijack distress allowance

Coverage amounts:
- Medical coverage: USD 50,000 to USD 5,00,000 (depending on plan)
- Baggage loss: USD 500 to USD 2,000
- Trip cancellation: Up to 100% of trip cost (subject to policy limit)
- Personal accident: USD 10,000 to USD 2,50,000

1.2 Domestic Travel Insurance
Covers risks within India:
- Medical emergencies during domestic travel
- Trip cancellation/curtailment
- Loss of baggage and personal belongings
- Flight delay compensation
- Missed connecting flights
- Hotel extension due to illness
- Emergency hotel accommodation

1.3 Student Travel Insurance
Specialized coverage for students studying abroad:
- Medical coverage (mandatory for most university admissions)
- Sponsor protection (covers education if sponsor dies/becomes disabled)
- Study interruption coverage
- Personal liability
- Compassionate visit by family member
- Tuition fee coverage for repeat year due to medical emergency

1.4 Corporate/Business Travel Insurance
Group travel insurance for corporate employees:
- Multi-trip annual policies available
- Covers business travel equipment (laptops, samples)
- Meeting cancellation coverage
- Emergency business document replacement

CHAPTER 2: VISA MANDATORY INSURANCE REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------------------------

2.1 Schengen Visa Travel Insurance
For travel to any of the 27 Schengen Area countries:
MANDATORY requirements:
- Minimum medical coverage: EUR 30,000 (approximately INR 27,00,000)
- Coverage must be valid for all Schengen states
- Policy must cover repatriation expenses and emergency medical evacuation
- Must not have any deductible/excess for medical claims
- Validity: Must cover the entire duration of stay + 15 days buffer

2.2 Other Visa Requirements
- USA (B1/B2): Travel insurance recommended but not mandatory
- UK: Not mandatory but strongly recommended
- Canada: Mandatory for Super Visa applicants — minimum CAD 100,000 coverage
- Australia: Mandatory OSHC (Overseas Student Health Cover) for student visa
- UAE: Mandatory health insurance for residence visa holders

CHAPTER 3: MEDICAL EMERGENCY COVERAGE
----------------------------------------

3.1 Inpatient Treatment Abroad
- Covers hospitalization expenses in the destination country
- No sub-limits on room rent in most international policies
- Emergency surgery and post-operative care included
- ICU charges covered within overall medical sum insured

3.2 Emergency Medical Evacuation
- Air ambulance charges for medical evacuation to nearest adequate hospital
- Repatriation to home country for continued treatment
- Mortal remains repatriation (in case of death during travel)
- Escort/companion travel expenses for accompanying family member

3.3 Pre-Existing Disease Coverage
- Most travel policies EXCLUDE pre-existing conditions
- Some premium plans offer limited PED coverage for acute emergencies
- Definition: "acute episode of pre-existing condition" means a sudden crisis
  that requires immediate medical attention and could not have been foreseen
- Chronic conditions management: NOT covered

3.4 Telemedicine Facility
Several travel insurers now offer:
- 24/7 telemedicine consultation in the traveler's language
- Initial medical assessment before hospital visit
- Prescription guidance and pharmacy coordination abroad
- Follow-up consultations during the trip

CHAPTER 4: TRIP CANCELLATION AND INTERRUPTION
------------------------------------------------

4.1 Covered Reasons for Trip Cancellation
A claim for trip cancellation is valid ONLY if caused by:
- Death, injury, or serious illness of insured or immediate family member
- Natural disasters at destination (earthquake, flood, volcanic eruption)
- Insured being summoned for jury duty or as court witness
- Terrorist incident at destination within 30 days before departure
- Government travel advisory against travel to the destination
- Insured's home damaged by fire, flood, or burglary within 72 hours of departure
- Visa rejection (covered under specific policies)

NOT covered:
- Change of mind or personal preference
- Work-related cancellation (unless covered by corporate policy)
- Pandemic/epidemic-related restrictions (policy-specific — check wording)
- Known or foreseeable events at the time of booking

4.2 Trip Cancellation Reimbursement
- Non-refundable travel costs: Flight tickets, hotel bookings, tour packages
- Maximum reimbursement: Up to the sum insured for trip cancellation
- Waiting period: Claims for cancellation within 24 hours of booking may not be covered
- Documentation required: Cancellation confirmation, medical certificates, police reports

4.3 Trip Curtailment
- Covers additional travel costs to return home early due to covered reasons
- Unused portion of travel arrangements is reimbursed
- Reasonable additional accommodation and transport costs covered

CHAPTER 5: BAGGAGE AND PERSONAL BELONGINGS
---------------------------------------------

5.1 Checked Baggage Loss
- Covers permanent loss of checked-in baggage
- Claim basis: Depreciated value of lost items (not replacement cost)
- Per-item sub-limit: Typically USD 200-500 per single item
- Total baggage coverage: USD 500 to USD 2,000 depending on plan
- Exclusions: Cash, securities, business documents, perishable items,
  contact lenses, artificial limbs, dental fittings

5.2 Baggage Delay
- Compensates for essential purchases when baggage is delayed
- Trigger: Delay beyond 12 hours (some policies: 6 hours)
- Coverage: USD 50-200 for essential clothing and toiletries
- Must provide: Delay certificate from airline, purchase receipts

5.3 Passport Loss
- Covers expenses for obtaining duplicate/emergency passport
- Travel and accommodation costs while waiting for replacement
- Typically covers: Passport fees, photographs, courier charges
- Maximum: USD 200-500

CHAPTER 6: CLAIM PROCESS FOR TRAVEL INSURANCE
-------------------------------------------------

6.1 Emergency During Travel
Step 1: Call the 24/7 emergency assistance helpline (number on insurance card)
Step 2: Assistance team coordinates hospital admission/cashless facility
Step 3: Keep all original bills, receipts, and medical reports
Step 4: For baggage loss: File PIR (Property Irregularity Report) with airline
Step 5: For theft: File police complaint at local police station

6.2 Required Documents (Post-Trip Claim)
- Duly filled claim form
- Original policy document/certificate
- Boarding passes and flight tickets
- Medical bills and reports (original)
- Hospital discharge summary
- Police report (for theft/robbery)
- Airline PIR report (for baggage claims)
- Hotel/transport receipts for additional expenses
- Visa rejection letter (for cancellation claims)
- Death certificate (for mortal remains repatriation claims)

6.3 Claim Settlement Timeline
- Claim intimation: Within 30 days of return to India
- Document submission: Within 60 days of claim intimation
- Insurer settlement: Within 30 days of complete document submission
- IRDAI mandate: Total settlement not to exceed 6 months

CHAPTER 7: EXCLUSIONS
------------------------

7.1 Standard Travel Insurance Exclusions
- Pre-existing medical conditions (unless specifically covered)
- Travel against medical advice
- Adventure sports/hazardous activities (unless add-on purchased)
  Excluded activities: bungee jumping, skydiving, paragliding, scuba diving
  below 30 meters, mountaineering above 4,000 meters
- War, civil unrest, or military action
- Nuclear or chemical contamination
- Self-inflicted injuries or suicide
- Travel under the influence of alcohol or drugs
- Professional sports participation
- Pregnancy-related expenses (unless complication during first 24 weeks)
- Dental treatment (unless emergency due to accident)
- Expenses for treatment that was the purpose of travel (medical tourism)
