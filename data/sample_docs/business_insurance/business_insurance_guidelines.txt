CHAPTER 1: GROUP INSURANCE PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------

1.1 Group Health Insurance (GHI)
Employer-sponsored health insurance covering employees and their dependents.

IRDAI Group Health Insurance Guidelines:
- Minimum group size: 20 members (IRDAI relaxed to 7 for micro/small enterprises)
- Employee contribution: Can be fully employer-funded or co-paid
- Sum insured range: INR 2,00,000 to INR 20,00,000
- Coverage: Same as individual health policies (hospitalization, pre/post care)
- Pre-existing diseases: Usually covered from Day 1 (no waiting period in group policies)
- Maternity coverage: Commonly included with sub-limits
- New joinee coverage: From date of joining (no initial waiting period)

1.2 Group Term Life Insurance (GTLI)
Employer-funded life insurance providing death benefit to employees.

Key Features:
- Minimum group size: 50 members (can be lower for specific schemes)
- Sum assured: Multiple of annual salary (1x to 10x, typically 2x-3x)
- Coverage: Death due to any cause (natural, accidental, illness)
- Additional coverage: Accidental death and disability rider
- Terminal illness benefit: Advance payment of 50% sum assured on terminal diagnosis
- Free cover limit: Sum assured up to which no medical examination required
  (Typically INR 50,00,000 - varies by insurer and group size)

1.3 Group Personal Accident Insurance
Covers employees for accidental death and disability:
- Death due to accident: 100% of sum insured
- Permanent total disability: 100% of sum insured
- Permanent partial disability: Percentage table based on nature of disability
- Temporary total disability: Weekly benefit (1% of sum insured per week, max 100 weeks)

CHAPTER 2: COMMERCIAL PROPERTY INSURANCE
-------------------------------------------

2.1 Standard Fire and Special Perils (SFSP) for Commercial Properties
Same base coverage as residential SFSP but with additional considerations:

Key Differences from Residential:
- Higher sum insured (typically crores of rupees)
- Stock-in-trade covered (raw materials, work-in-progress, finished goods)
- Machinery and equipment specifically valued
- Business interruption add-on available
- More stringent risk assessment and inspection requirements

2.2 Industrial All Risk (IAR) Policy
Broader coverage than SFSP for large industrial/commercial entities:
- All risks coverage (any accidental loss or damage not specifically excluded)
- Machinery breakdown included
- Business interruption consequential loss
- Higher limits and broader coverage scope
- Suitable for large manufacturing, IT companies, retail chains

2.3 Office Package Policy (Vyapar Suraksha)
Comprehensive package for offices and small businesses:
- Building and contents coverage
- Burglary and theft
- Money in transit and on premises
- Employee fidelity guarantee
- Electronic equipment coverage
- Public liability
- Personal accident for proprietor/partners

CHAPTER 3: LIABILITY INSURANCE
---------------------------------

3.1 Professional Liability Insurance (Errors and Omissions / E&O)
Covers professionals against claims of negligence or errors:

Applicable Professions:
- IT companies and consultants
- Chartered Accountants
- Doctors and hospitals (Medical Malpractice)
- Lawyers and legal firms
- Architects and engineers
- Financial advisors and brokers

Coverage includes:
- Legal defense costs
- Damages/compensation awarded by court
- Settlement amounts (with insurer's consent)
- Regulatory investigation costs

Exclusions:
- Intentional acts or fraud
- Criminal prosecution
- Known claims before policy inception
- Contractual liability (unless arising from negligence)

3.2 Directors and Officers (D&O) Liability Insurance
Covers directors and officers against personal liability for decisions:
- Wrongful act by director/officer in their capacity
- Shareholder derivative suits
- Regulatory investigations (SEBI, IRDAI, CCI)
- Employment practices liability claims
- Entity coverage for corporate indemnification
- Coverage extends to past, present, and future D&Os

3.3 Product Liability Insurance
Covers manufacturers/sellers against claims for defective products:
- Bodily injury caused by defective product
- Property damage caused by defective product
- Legal defense costs
- Recall expenses (with specific endorsement)
- Mandatory for e-commerce sellers and manufacturers under Consumer Protection Act 2019

CHAPTER 4: MARINE INSURANCE
------------------------------

4.1 Marine Cargo Insurance
Covers goods during transit by sea, air, rail, or road.

Types of Coverage:
a) Institute Cargo Clause (A) — All Risks:
   Covers all risks of loss or damage except specifically excluded perils
   Most comprehensive coverage

b) Institute Cargo Clause (B) — Named Perils (Broader):
   Covers specific named perils including fire, vessel stranding, overturning,
   earthquake, volcanic eruption, washing overboard

c) Institute Cargo Clause (C) — Named Perils (Basic):
   Covers basic named perils only: fire, vessel stranding, overturning,
   collision, discharge at port of distress

4.2 Marine Hull Insurance
Covers the vessel/ship itself against damage:
- Total loss (actual or constructive)
- Partial damage to hull and machinery
- Salvage charges
- General average contributions
- Third-party collision liability

4.3 Inland Transit Insurance
Covers goods moving within India:
- Rail transit
- Road transit (truck, tempo, courier)
- Inland waterway transit
- Covers: Fire, overturning, collision, theft, natural calamities
- Annual open covers available for regular shippers

CHAPTER 5: KEYMAN INSURANCE
------------------------------

5.1 What is Keyman Insurance?
Insurance taken by a company on the life of a key employee/executive whose
death or disability would cause significant financial loss to the company.

IRDAI Guidelines for Keyman Insurance:
- The company is the proposer and premium payer
- The key person is the life assured
- The company is the nominee/beneficiary
- Sum assured: Based on the financial loss estimated from the key person's absence
- Maximum sum assured: 10x annual compensation of the key person OR
  Expected future profit contribution, whichever is reasonable

5.2 Tax Treatment of Keyman Insurance
- Premium paid by the company: Tax-deductible as business expense (Section 37)
- Maturity/death claim proceeds: Taxable as income of the company (revenue receipt)
- If policy is assigned to the key person (on retirement):
  The surrender value at assignment is taxable as perquisite for the employee
  Subsequent maturity: Taxable under Section 17(1) as salary

5.3 Assignment of Keyman Policy
- Company can assign the policy to the keyman employee at retirement
- Assignment must be with board resolution
- Post-assignment: Employee becomes the policyholder and pays own premiums
- Premium paid by employee post-assignment: Eligible for 80C deduction

CHAPTER 6: WORKERS' COMPENSATION INSURANCE
---------------------------------------------

6.1 Legal Requirement
As per the Employees' Compensation Act, 1923 (formerly Workmen's Compensation Act):
- Employers are liable to pay compensation for work-related injuries/death
- Insurance is MANDATORY for certain categories of workers/establishments
- Covers: Death, permanent total/partial disability, temporary disability

6.2 Compensation Amounts (Updated as per 2024 amendments)
- Death: 50% of monthly wages × relevant factor (based on age) OR INR 1,20,000
  whichever is higher (minimum compensation as per current rates)
- Permanent total disability: Same as death compensation
- Permanent partial disability: Percentage of disability × permanent total disability compensation
- Temporary disability: 25% of monthly wages for disabled period

6.3 Employer's Liability Policy
- Covers the employer's legal liability to pay compensation
- Coverage as per Employees' Compensation Act + Fatal Accidents Act
- Premium based on number of employees, nature of work, and claims experience
- Also covers legal defense costs for disputed claims

CHAPTER 7: CLAIMS PROCEDURE FOR CORPORATE POLICIES
------------------------------------------------------

7.1 Group Health Insurance Claims
- Cashless facility at network hospitals (same as individual policies)
- TPA manages claims processing
- HR department coordinates between employee and TPA
- Documents: Employee ID, policy certificate, standard health claim documents

7.2 Commercial Property Claims
- Immediate intimation to insurer (within 24 hours of loss)
- FIR for theft/burglary/malicious damage
- Surveyor appointed by insurer for claims above INR 50,000
- Detailed stock register required for stock-in-trade claims
- Business interruption claims: Auditor's certificate for loss of profit

7.3 Marine Cargo Claims
- Survey at destination before taking delivery (if damage suspected)
- Note damage on the delivery receipt
- File claim with insurer within 7 days of taking delivery
- Documents: Bill of Lading, Invoice, Packing List, Survey Report, Damage Photos
