CHAPTER 1: TYPES OF MOTOR INSURANCE
--------------------------------------

1.1 Third-Party Liability Insurance (Mandatory)
As per Section 146 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988:
- Third-party motor insurance is MANDATORY for all motor vehicles in India
- Covers liability to third parties for bodily injury or death
- Also covers property damage to third-party property up to INR 7,50,000
- Driving without valid insurance is a punishable offense:
  First offense: Fine up to INR 2,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 months
  Subsequent offenses: Fine up to INR 4,000 and/or imprisonment up to 3 months

1.2 Comprehensive Insurance (Optional but Recommended)
Covers:
- Third-party liability (as above)
- Own damage (OD) to the insured vehicle
- Theft of the vehicle
- Fire, explosion, or self-ignition damage
- Natural calamities: earthquake, flood, storm, hurricane, cyclone
- Man-made calamities: riot, strike, malicious damage, terrorism
- Personal accident cover for owner-driver: INR 15,00,000 (mandatory add-on)

1.3 Standalone Own-Damage (OD) Policy
As per IRDAI Notification dated 01 August 2020:
- Vehicle owners can purchase separate OD policy from any insurer
- TP and OD need NOT be from the same insurer
- OD policy duration: 1 year (renewable annually)
- TP policy duration: 3 years for new cars, 5 years for new two-wheelers

CHAPTER 2: INSURED DECLARED VALUE (IDV)
------------------------------------------

2.1 IDV Calculation
IDV is the maximum amount payable in case of total loss/theft of the vehicle.

For vehicles up to 5 years old:
IDV = Manufacturer's listed selling price - Depreciation

Depreciation Schedule (IRDAI prescribed):
| Vehicle Age | Depreciation % |
|-------------|----------------|
| Not exceeding 6 months | 5% |
| 6 months to 1 year | 15% |
| 1 year to 2 years | 20% |
| 2 years to 3 years | 30% |
| 3 years to 4 years | 40% |
| 4 years to 5 years | 50% |

For vehicles older than 5 years:
IDV is determined by mutual agreement between insurer and insured, based on
physical inspection and market conditions.

2.2 IDV for Accessories
- Electrical/electronic accessories: Declared separately, IDV calculated with same depreciation
- Non-electrical accessories (e.g., CNG kit): Declared separately with depreciation
- Bi-fuel kits: Must be declared and separate premium charged

CHAPTER 3: NO-CLAIM BONUS (NCB)
----------------------------------

3.1 NCB Slab (IRDAI Standard)
NCB is a discount on the OD premium for claim-free years:

| Claim-Free Years | NCB Discount |
|-----------------|--------------|
| After 1 year | 20% |
| After 2 years | 25% |
| After 3 years | 35% |
| After 4 years | 45% |
| After 5+ years | 50% |

3.2 NCB Transfer Rules
- NCB belongs to the INSURED PERSON, not the vehicle
- NCB can be transferred when the insured buys a new vehicle
- NCB cannot be transferred to another person (except legal heir in case of death)
- NCB is lost if a claim is made during the policy year
- NCB can be protected with an NCB Protector add-on (claim without losing NCB)

3.3 NCB Proof for Transfer
- NCB certificate from the previous insurer
- Self-declaration of NCB (accepted by most insurers)
- Verification through the IIB (Insurance Information Bureau) portal

CHAPTER 4: CLAIMS PROCEDURE
------------------------------

4.1 Accident Claim Process
Step 1: Inform the insurer immediately (within 24 hours of the accident)
Step 2: File an FIR with the local police (mandatory for third-party injury/death)
Step 3: Do NOT move the vehicle (for major accidents) until surveyor inspects
Step 4: Insurer appoints a surveyor to assess the damage
Step 5: Get the vehicle repaired at an approved garage (for cashless claims)
Step 6: Submit required documents:
  - Duly filled claim form
  - FIR / police complaint copy
  - Driving license of the driver at the time of accident
  - Registration Certificate (RC) copy
  - Insurance policy copy
  - Photographs of damaged vehicle
  - Repair estimate from garage
  - Original repair bills and payment receipts

4.2 Cashless Repair at Network Garages
- Insurer settles the bill directly with the network garage
- Policyholder pays only the deductible/excess amount
- Claim approval typically within 4-6 hours for minor repairs
- Major damage: Surveyor visit within 48 hours

4.3 Theft Claim Process
Step 1: File FIR with police within 24 hours of theft
Step 2: Inform the insurer within 24 hours
Step 3: Provide: FIR copy, policy document, RC, all sets of car keys
Step 4: If vehicle not traced in 90 days — obtain Non-Traceable Certificate from police
Step 5: Submit final claim documents including Non-Traceable Certificate
Step 6: Transfer vehicle ownership to insurer (via Form 28, 29, 30, 35)
Step 7: Insurer settles the claim at IDV minus depreciation

4.4 Depreciation on Parts (During Repairs)
IRDAI-prescribed depreciation on replaced parts:
| Part Type | Depreciation |
|-----------|-------------|
| Rubber, nylon, plastic parts | 50% |
| Fiber glass components | 30% |
| Glass | Nil |
| Wooden parts | Nil |
| Metal body parts | Nil (if vehicle < 5 years) |
| Batteries | 50% |
| Tyres and tubes | 50% |

CHAPTER 5: ADD-ON COVERS
---------------------------

5.1 Available Add-Ons (Optional)
- Zero Depreciation Cover: Full claim amount without depreciation deduction
- Engine Protection Cover: Covers engine/gearbox damage due to water ingression
- Road Side Assistance (RSA): Towing, battery jumpstart, flat tyre, fuel delivery
- Return to Invoice (RTI): Covers the difference between IDV and invoice price
- NCB Protector: Protects NCB even if a claim is made
- Key Replacement Cover: Covers loss/damage of car keys
- Personal Belongings Cover: Covers loss of personal items from the vehicle
- Consumables Cover: Covers consumables used during repair (nuts, bolts, oil, etc.)

5.2 Personal Accident Cover (Mandatory)
As per IRDAI circular dated 20 August 2019:
- Owner-driver personal accident cover of INR 15,00,000 is mandatory
- Covers death or permanent disability due to motor vehicle accident
- Can be purchased as standalone PA cover if not taken with motor insurance
- Paid driver PA cover: Optional add-on available

CHAPTER 6: RENEWAL AND BREAK-IN POLICY
-----------------------------------------

6.1 Renewal Norms
- Policy renewal: Must be done before or on the expiry date
- IRDAI allows online renewal without physical inspection if renewed within expiry
- Premium changes: Based on annual IRDAI tariff revision + claims experience

6.2 Break-in Policy (Lapsed Insurance)
- If motor insurance has expired (even by 1 day): Vehicle inspection is mandatory
- Inspection can be done at insurer's office or through mobile app (with geo-tagged photos)
- Break-in period: NCB is lost if policy is not renewed within 90 days of expiry
- Third-party insurance cannot have a break: Driving without TP insurance is illegal
