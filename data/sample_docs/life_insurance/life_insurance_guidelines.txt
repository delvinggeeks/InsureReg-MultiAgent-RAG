CHAPTER 1: PRODUCT CATEGORIES AND DEFINITIONS
----------------------------------------------

1.1 Term Life Insurance
Term life insurance provides pure risk coverage for a specified period. If the policyholder
dies during the term, the sum assured is paid to the nominee. No maturity benefit is payable
if the policyholder survives the term.

Key Features:
- Minimum sum assured: INR 25,00,000 (as per IRDAI guidelines for online term plans)
- Maximum entry age: 65 years
- Minimum policy term: 5 years
- Maximum policy term: Up to age 85 (or 40 years, whichever is lower)
- Premium payment modes: Annual, Semi-Annual, Quarterly, Monthly

1.2 Endowment Plans
Endowment plans combine risk coverage with savings. They pay the sum assured on death
during the policy term OR the maturity benefit on surviving the term.

Guaranteed Surrender Value (GSV):
- After 2 full years of premium payment, the policy acquires a surrender value
- GSV = 30% of total premiums paid (excluding first year premium) for traditional plans
- Special Surrender Value (SSV) = Paid-up value × (Remaining term factor)

1.3 Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs)
ULIPs are market-linked life insurance products where premiums are invested in equity,
debt, or balanced funds chosen by the policyholder.

IRDAI ULIP Regulations (2019 amended):
- Minimum lock-in period: 5 years from date of inception
- Maximum fund management charge: 1.35% per annum
- Mortality charges must be based on published mortality tables
- Policyholder can switch between funds — 4 free switches per year
- Partial withdrawal allowed after 5 years lock-in period

1.4 Pension and Annuity Plans
These plans provide regular income after retirement. The accumulation phase builds a
corpus, and the annuity phase provides regular pension payments.

IRDAI Requirements:
- Minimum annuity rate must be guaranteed at inception
- At least 1/3 of the corpus can be commuted (taken as lump sum)
- Remaining 2/3 must be used to purchase an annuity
- Joint life annuity option must be available

CHAPTER 2: FREE-LOOK PERIOD AND CANCELLATION
----------------------------------------------

2.1 Free-Look Period
As per IRDAI Circular No. IRDA/Life/CIR/GDL/077/02/2024:
- The policyholder has 15 days (30 days for distance marketing/online purchases) from
  receipt of the policy document to review the terms and conditions
- If not satisfied, the policyholder can return the policy and receive a refund
- Refund amount = Premium paid - Stamp duty - Medical examination charges - 
  Proportionate risk premium for the coverage period

2.2 Cooling Off Period for ULIPs
For ULIPs, the free-look cancellation value = Fund value as on date of cancellation +
Charges levied through cancellation of units - Non-refundable charges as per IRDAI norms

CHAPTER 3: GRACE PERIOD AND POLICY REVIVAL
--------------------------------------------

3.1 Grace Period for Premium Payment
- Annual mode: 30 days from premium due date
- Semi-annual mode: 30 days from premium due date
- Quarterly mode: 30 days from premium due date
- Monthly mode: 15 days from premium due date

During the grace period, the policy remains in full force. If the insured dies during the
grace period, the death benefit is payable after deducting the outstanding premium.

3.2 Policy Lapse and Revival
- A policy lapses if premium is not paid within the grace period
- Revival is allowed within 5 years from the date of first unpaid premium (for traditional plans)
- Revival requirements: Application form, health declaration, payment of all unpaid premiums
  with interest, and possible medical examination
- Revival interest rate: Prevailing rate as per company board approval (typically 9-10% p.a.)

CHAPTER 4: NOMINATION AND ASSIGNMENT
--------------------------------------

4.1 Nomination (Section 39 of Insurance Act 1938)
- Any policyholder can nominate one or more persons to receive the policy proceeds
- Nominee must be a family member or legal heir
- For ULIP and pension plans, specific nomination rules apply
- Nomination can be changed any number of times during the policy term
- Written notice must be given to the insurer for nomination change

4.2 Beneficial Nominee vs Collector Nominee
As per the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act, 2015:
- Beneficial Nominee: Spouse, children, and parents — they receive the proceeds as
  absolute owners
- Collector Nominee: Other nominees who hold the proceeds in trust for legal heirs

4.3 Assignment (Section 38 of Insurance Act 1938)
- Policy can be assigned (transferred) to another person
- Assignment must be made by endorsement on the policy or by separate deed
- Notice of assignment must be given to the insurer
- Assignee gets all rights, title, and interest in the policy

CHAPTER 5: DEATH CLAIM SETTLEMENT
------------------------------------

5.1 Claims Settlement Timeline (IRDAI mandate)
- Simple death claims: Must be settled within 30 days of receipt of all documents
- If investigation is required: Must be completed within 90 days from claim intimation
- If investigation reveals no adverse findings: Claim must be paid within 30 days of
  investigation completion
- Total period from claim intimation to settlement: Not to exceed 6 months

5.2 Required Documents for Death Claim
- Original or certified copy of death certificate
- Policy document (original)
- Claim form (Form A) duly filled by nominee/claimant
- NEFT details of claimant's bank account
- KYC documents of claimant (Aadhaar, PAN)
- FIR copy (in case of accidental/unnatural death)
- Post-mortem report (if applicable)
- Hospital records (in case of death due to illness)

5.3 Early Death Claims (Within 3 years of policy inception)
As per Section 45 of Insurance Act 1938 (amended):
- Claims within 3 years: Insurer can investigate for misrepresentation/fraud
- After 3 years: Policy cannot be called into question on any grounds whatsoever
- Insurer must prove fraud with specific evidence; mere suspicion is not sufficient

CHAPTER 6: TAX BENEFITS
-------------------------

6.1 Section 80C of Income Tax Act
- Premium paid for life insurance qualifies for deduction under Section 80C
- Maximum deduction: INR 1,50,000 per financial year
- For policies issued after 01.04.2012: Premium should not exceed 10% of sum assured

6.2 Section 10(10D) — Tax-Free Maturity
- Maturity proceeds are tax-free under Section 10(10D) if:
  - For policies issued after 01.04.2012: Annual premium does not exceed 10% of sum assured
  - For policies issued after 01.04.2023 (Budget 2023): If total premium exceeds 
    INR 5,00,000 in aggregate across all policies, the maturity is taxable

6.3 Section 80CCC — Pension Plans
- Contribution to pension/annuity plans qualifies for deduction under Section 80CCC
- Maximum deduction combined with 80C: INR 1,50,000
- Pension received is taxable as income in the year of receipt
